# Bangkok's 0.02 g quake hid long-period resonance

The best-supported free-field peak ground acceleration at the State Audit Office (SAO) site in Chatuchak on 28 March 2025 is **about 0.02 g (roughly 20–24 cm/s²), with PGV of about 10–14 cm/s**. That comes from the Thai Meteorological Department (TMD) basin stations, all about 600 km from the rupture. No instrument stood at the site, and no TMD record supports the 0.08 g figure some papers quote. What made the event damaging was frequency content, not PGA. Every Bangkok spectrum shows soil amplification in **two bands, 1–3 s and 5–7 s**, with reported peaks near **1.6, 2.8 and 6.3 s**, Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) long-period **Class 3** motion around 6 s, and shaking that lasted more than a minute (up to about two). For a 137 m, 33-storey reinforced-concrete core building, Bangkok's measured period–height relations and the code formulas give a small-amplitude fundamental period of **about 2.7 s (range 2.0–3.3 s)**. Reported post-event period lengthening of up to 40% pushes the effective period during shaking to roughly 3–3.8 s. The official investigation published no site record, spectrum or building period. It blamed capacity: shear walls at floors 1–4 failed, concrete was below specification, the design did not comply with the law, and link-beam anchorage was short. For the user's OpenSees models, the PGA and PGV inputs are well supported. Three things are not: the target spectrum's shape, especially the 5–7 s plateau of 0.019–0.024 g, which looks too low; the 60 s duration, which is at the short end; and the 6.1 s and 7.65 s as-built periods, which are more than twice the empirical estimate. All three need checking before the models are used to argue for or against resonance.

A caveat applies throughout. The underlying research could not open the primary PDFs (the Springer and E3S papers, the TMD and USGS pages), so the numbers below come from abstracts and search-engine extracts. Check every value against the original before publication.

## Five TMD basin stations recorded about 0.02 g, and none supports 0.08 g

All recorded Bangkok motion comes from **five TMD strong-motion stations in the basin**: four free-field stations (PTNA in the north, PWSA central, KMUA south, BKKA southeast) and a borehole sensor, BKSI, at 30 m depth, 7 km from PWSA. The definitive analysis is by Ornthammarath, Warnitchai, Maruyama, Pornsopin and Yamazaki in the *Bulletin of Earthquake Engineering*. It states that "most strong motion sensors in Bangkok reported PGA and PGV from this event of **0.02 g and 10 cm/s**", the largest ever recorded in the capital ([Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)). Under the central city, where PWSA and BKSI sit, bedrock lies **700–900 m** deep. Under KMUA and BKKA it is about 530–560 m ([Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)).

PWSA is the best-documented record. Maruyama's report gives its JMA instrumental intensity as 3.5 (JMA 4). It also notes that the three channels may not have been time-synchronised, and its table extract was garbled, so the component labels are uncertain ([Maruyama PWSA report](http://ares.tu.chiba-u.jp/marulab/survey/report/PWSA.pdf)). A third, unattributed variant gives PWSA as 0.019 / 0.023 / 0.009 g (NS/EW/UD), which agrees to within rounding. No public station-level data came from Chulalongkorn, Kasetsart, AIT or the Department of Mineral Resources (DMR). The **SAO site at about 13.80 N, 100.55 E** lies in the north-central basin, between the central PWSA/BKSI pair and the northern PTNA, and all three read 0.02 g. The basin amplifies relative to outside sites by a factor of **about 3 in PGA and 4–5 in horizontal PGV** ([Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)). Warnitchai puts PGA at 3–6 mg outside the basin and **about 20 mg inside** ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/bangkok/40064540)). Interpolating across the three neighbouring stations, the SAO's free-field PGA was very likely 0.02 g (15–25 cm/s²) and its PGV 10–14 cm/s. BKSI's value is a 30 m-depth reading, not a surface one, so PWSA is the better surface proxy.

The **0.08 g figure** traces to a single E3S Web of Conferences paper (Ismail et al., ICDMM 2025). It says TMD "recorded a maximum ground acceleration of about 0.08 g" with MMI IV–V in Bangkok ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area); [E3S PDF](https://www.e3s-conferences.org/articles/e3sconf/pdf/2025/77/e3sconf_icdmm2025_09003.pdf)). The same paper undercuts itself. It also cites "a TMD report of roughly 0.04–0.06 g", and it frames 0.08 g as what "field observations and numerical simulations show... can reach" in soft soil. That is a modelled or asserted value, not an instrument reading. TMD's own five instruments peaked at 0.02 g, so 0.08 g is **three to four times every recorded value** and should not be used as site PGA. The likeliest explanations are confusion with a spectral ordinate in the 1–3 s band (where 0.08 g is plausible, as discussed below), a provincial station nearer the source, or a simulation result. None of these is confirmed. USGS ShakeMap values for Bangkok could not be retrieved. Secondary sources put Bangkok at **MMI V** ([ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950576325000017)), and Guy Carpenter warns that ShakeMap "may not adequately capture the effects of basin amplification" in Bangkok ([Guy Carpenter](https://www.guycarp.com/content/dam/guycarp-rebrand/insights-images/2025/04/04_03_2025_MM_EQ_Report_20250403_v2.pdf)). MMI V normally implies more than 2 %g. The gap is consistent with intensity being driven by long-period, long-duration motion rather than by PGA.

## Basin resonance at 1–3 s and 5–7 s set the frequency content

The defining finding is spectral. "All recorded response spectra in Bangkok show long period soil amplification within the period range of **1–3 s and 5–7 s**." That amplification is "almost **10 times**" the level at stiff-soil stations at similar distance ([Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)). A 2026 tall-building study in the *Journal of Earthquake Engineering* goes further: "although the peak ground acceleration was only about 0.02 g, spectral amplitudes in the long-period range were amplified by **more than ten times**" ([T&F JEE 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969)). Warnitchai locates the amplified peaks at **about 1.6 s, 2.8 s and 6.3 s** ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/bangkok/40064540); [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/bangkoks-soft-soil-basin-could-amplify-earthquake-shaking-by-3-6-times/)). Maruyama et al. apply the JMA long-period intensity scale and find **Class 3** motion with dominant periods around 6 s: "At BKSI and PWSA, the Sva exceeded the Class 3 threshold around a 6-second period" ([EARTH Digital Library, TSER2025](https://earth.nrct.go.th/EarthLib/item.php?id=32)). Here Sva is the absolute velocity response spectrum at 5% damping. PWSA's velocity response spectrum peaks at **6.0–6.5 s** ([Maruyama PWSA report](http://ares.tu.chiba-u.jp/marulab/survey/report/PWSA.pdf)). No numerical Sa ordinates at specific periods were retrievable. They exist only in figures of the BEE paper, the Maruyama report and Warnitchai's May 2025 slides.

The JMA classification still allows a useful order-of-magnitude check, and this one is the report's own inference. Class 3 conventionally corresponds to Sva of 50–100 cm/s. At T = 6 s that converts to a pseudo-spectral acceleration of about **0.05–0.11 g** (Sa = 2π/T × Sv), or a spectral displacement of roughly 0.5–0.9 m. Sva is an absolute rather than a pseudo velocity, so treat this as approximate. Even so, it shows the 6 s ordinate at PWSA and BKSI was probably several times the 0.02 g PGA. That is how a basin resonance driven by a ~10–14 cm/s PGV can behave over a long, narrowband record.

The two amplified bands match Bangkok's two measured site periods. The **shallow soft-clay mode** is about 0.7–1.2 s. Microtremor surveys at more than 150 sites found 0.8–1.2 s near the Gulf ([Tuladhar et al. 2004](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/eqe.345)), and a five-site array study found 0.68–0.86 s ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2021.651902/full)). The clay is 15–20 m thick in central Bangkok and thins "more rapidly towards the north" ([Tuladhar et al. via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227669148_Seismic_microzonation_of_the_greater_Bangkok_area_using_microtremor_observations)). Chatuchak's shallow period is therefore probably 0.6–1.0 s, though no site-specific measurement exists. The **deep-basin mode** is 2–5 s by H/V at 115 sites ([Pananont et al., SRL 2025](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/shear-wave-velocity-structure-and-site-response-analysis-of-the-b/)), and earthquake H/V inside the basin peaks near 5 s ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2021.651902/full)). In 2025 that mode appeared as the 5–7 s band. The Ismail paper's claim of a 1.8–2.5 s soft-clay period is the outlier ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)). For 15–30 m of clay with Vs ≈ 100 m/s, the quarter-wavelength period 4H/Vs is only 0.6–1.2 s. The 1.6 s and 2.8 s peaks of 2025 more likely reflect intermediate-depth layers or nonlinear softening than the surface clay alone.

Set against the code, the record is moderate in the building-period range and exceptional only at very long periods. The DPT 1301/1302 design spectrum "is greater than that of recorded spectral ordinates between 0 and 5 s", but "the recorded spectrum does exceed the design spectrum for periods above 5 s". The reference Bangkok spectrum has **Sa(1 s) = 0.20 g at DBE and 0.30 g at MCE** ([Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)). The 2018 code revision raised Bangkok's Sa at 1 s by about 25% but **cut it by 28% at 6 s** relative to the 2009 code ([Poovarodom, SPU dspace](https://dspace.spu.ac.th/bitstream/123456789/5894/1/ACEE0232_Poovarodom_A%20New%20Earthquake%20Resistant%20Design%20Standard%20for%20Buildings%20in%20Thailand.pdf)), which is the band the 2025 motion exceeded. The design zone covering Chatuchak was not confirmed.

Duration is the least-constrained parameter. **No published D5-95 or Arias-based duration exists for the 2025 Bangkok records.** The BEE paper did not evaluate duration ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)). TMD reported shaking of "more than one minute" (same source), and Warnitchai says shaking lasted **up to about 2 minutes** ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/bangkok/40064540)). Earlier distant events give a scaling: significant duration at the basin station TMDA was about **1.5 times** that at rock or stiff sites, and 1–3.3 s content dominated for 3–4 minutes after the surface waves arrived ([Ornthammarath et al. 2023](https://geoenvironmental-disasters.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40677-023-00259-0)). Combined with a rupture of about 80 s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Myanmar_earthquake)), this makes a Bangkok significant duration of more than 60 s and plausibly 1–3 minutes the reasonable inference.

## A 137 m core-wall tower in Bangkok sways at about 2.7 s

No measured, modelled or officially reported period exists for the SAO building ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40051947); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/03/20/thai-audit-office-releases-report-on-building-collapse-cites-four-main-causes)). The "8 seconds" often quoted is the duration of the collapse, not a vibration period ([BKK Tribune](https://bkktribune.com/8-critical-seconds-how-the-30-storey-building-collapsed-into-a-layer-cake/)). The best estimate therefore rests on Bangkok's own measured buildings. Ambient-vibration surveys cover about **50 RC buildings 20–210 m tall** ([13WCEE Paper 1264](https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/wcee/article/13_1264.pdf)) and **98 RC buildings 7–142 m tall** ([Ornthammarath et al., J. Building Eng. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352710224032595)). The second database tops out at almost exactly the SAO's height, and its authors report that measured periods "correlate well" with the DPT code estimate. They also report that soil–structure interaction makes Bangkok periods longer than literature values for comparable RC buildings. A Thai conference paper tabulates the Poovarodom–Warnitchai relation as **T = 0.02H** ([NCCE 28](https://conference.thaince.org/index.php/ncce28/article/download/2168/1141/33067)). The Thai standard allows T = 0.02H or an eigenvalue analysis, but the period used for design may not exceed **1.5 × 0.02H** ([Engineering Journal](https://engj.org/index.php/ej/article/view/4685/1472)). Warnitchai's public talks pair 24-storey buildings with about 2.4 s and 60-storey towers with about 6 s ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/bangkoks-soft-soil-basin-could-amplify-earthquake-shaking-by-3-6-times/)), roughly T ≈ N/10. His slides present 2.4 s as an illustration, not a survey statistic ([BKNIX slides](https://peeringforum.bknix.co.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/1.0-Bangkok-EQ-BKNIK-PF-22May2025.pdf)).

|---|---|

| Eurocode 8, Ct = 0.05 (walls; formally limited to H ≤ 40 m) | 2.00 s |
| Thai DPT 1301/1302-61, T = 0.02H (Bangkok-validated) | **2.74 s** |

| Eurocode 8, Ct = 0.075 (RC frames, for reference) | 3.00 s |

| ASCE 7 RC moment frame (Ct = 0.0466, x = 0.9; not applicable) | 3.90 s |
| DPT upper limit, 1.5 × 0.02H | **4.11 s** |

The code coefficients above are standard values applied by the research team, not quoted from a fetched code text. The DPT 0.02H clause and its 1.5 multiplier are confirmed only through one secondary paper ([Engineering Journal](https://engj.org/index.php/ej/article/view/4685/1472)). Together they give a **small-amplitude central estimate of about 2.7 s, with a plausible range of 2.0–3.3 s**. During shaking the period lengthens. The JEE 2026 study found "**permanent period elongation of up to 40%**" in several Bangkok RC high-rises after this event ([T&F JEE 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969)). Applied to 2.7 s, that gives an effective period of about 3–3.8 s. Several features of the SAO would push its period toward the upper end. It was under construction, so it had no façade or partitions to add small-amplitude stiffness. Its post-tensioned flat slab provides little frame action. The core walls were thinned from **30 cm to 25 cm** ([Thailand Construction](https://thailand-construction.com/reduced-thickness-of-elevator-shaft-walls-now-in-focus-on-bangkoks-collapsed-state-audit-office-building/)). And the eccentric rear core couples translation with torsion ([BKK Tribune](https://bkktribune.com/govt-appointed-panel-focuses-its-probe-on-design-of-collapsed-sao-building/)). None of these effects has been quantified publicly. Even so, a period of 3–4 s is defensible for the as-built flexible system, while 6 s or more would put a 33-storey building on a par with Bangkok's 60-storey towers.

## Investigators blamed capacity and published no demand values

The official probe ran four independent university simulations (Chulalongkorn, KMITL, Kasetsart and KMUTT) under a DPT-led committee ([Thai PBS Verify](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/verify/en/content/14616)). It did **not publish the ground-motion record, PGA, spectrum or modal periods** it used. Its finding, announced on 30 June 2025 and repeated in the SAO's March 2026 report, was that "the collapse began in the lower part of the building (**floors 1–4**) due to earthquake-induced shear acting on the shear walls (lift walls and stair walls) until failure" ([ThaiPublica](https://thaipublica.org/2025/06/prime-minister-reveals-audit-office-collapse-report/)). It named four causes. The first was the shear failure of those lower-floor walls. The second was core-wall concrete averaging below the acceptance criterion. The third was detailed drawings that did not comply with the law, "resulting in the building being able to withstand less force than required by law". The fourth was link-beam to shear-wall bar embedment shorter than legally required ([Nation, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.nationthailand.com/blogs/news/general/40064045); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/03/20/thai-audit-office-releases-report-on-building-collapse-cites-four-main-causes)). Thai PBS Verify reports that the design referenced ACI 318-08 and the superseded DPT 1302-52, and that the collapse was "not caused by the earthquake alone" ([Thai PBS Verify](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/verify/en/content/14616)).

Engineering association figures, citing Council of Engineers tests, report that **37.8% of wall samples from the 500 ksc-specified tower fell below 75% of specification** ([Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5259494)). The panel's interim focus was the rear-offset core and cyclic torsion ([BKK Tribune](https://bkktribune.com/govt-appointed-panel-focuses-its-probe-on-design-of-collapsed-sao-building/)). Worsak Kanok-Nukulchai's video analysis likewise placed the start of the failure at the eccentric lift core, with torsion and diagonal column fractures ([Isranews](https://www.isranews.org/article/isranews-article/137265-wrs-4.html)). The Ismail paper attributes the collapse to resonance plus poor construction ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)), but resonance is not among the official causes. The SAO was the only building in Bangkok to collapse, although at least three others suffered similar core-wall damage ([Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40048611)). The official logic is about capacity, not demand: a compliant building should have survived. That fits the BEE finding that the record sat below the design spectrum across 0–5 s.

## Model PGA matches the record, but spectral shape, duration and periods need rework

The user's model inputs line up against the research findings as follows.

| Parameter | Model input | Best-supported research value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| PGA | 0.02 g | 0.02 g (PWSA peak horizontal 0.024 g) | Consistent; keep |
| PGV | ~10 cm/s | 10–14 cm/s (PWSA 13.9 EW, 10.8 NS) | Consistent; consider 12–14 cm/s for the stronger component |
| Short-period spectral peak | 0.08 g at 1.3 s | Amplified band 1–3 s, peaks ~1.6 s and ~2.8 s; below DPT DBE (0.20 g at 1 s) | Amplitude plausible but unverified; peak period too short; second peak at ~2.8 s missing |
| Long-period ordinates | Slow decay 0.024–0.019 g over 5–7 s | Distinct 5–7 s amplified band peaking ~6.0–6.5 s; JMA Class 3 at PWSA/BKSI; record exceeds DPT spectrum above 5 s | Probably too low, by a factor of about 2–5 if Class 3 holds |
| Strong-shaking duration | ~60 s | ">1 minute" (TMD), "up to ~2 minutes" (Warnitchai); no published D5-95 | Low end; extend and test sensitivity |
| As-built fundamental period | 6.1 s (nonlinear), 7.65 s (elastic cracked) | ~2.7 s small-amplitude (2.0–3.3 s); ~3–3.8 s with 40% elongation; DPT cap 4.11 s | 2.2–2.8 times the empirical estimate; verify |
| Proposed beam-slab period | 4.2 s (nonlinear), 4.96 s (elastic cracked) | Should fall below the as-built value and near 0.02H | Above the DPT cap; verify |

The PGA and PGV calibration is sound, and the 0.08 g spectral peak is not ruled out. It implies Sa/PGA = 4 at 1.3 s, which narrowband soft-clay resonance can produce, and it stays below the 0.20 g DBE ordinate, as the BEE comparison requires. Its value is not traceable to any published ordinate, though, and it coincides suspiciously with the unsupported "0.08 g PGA" figure. The target should be checked against the digitized PWSA and BKSI spectra in the BEE paper's figures, and its peak moved to about 1.6 s with a second lobe near 2.8 s.

The more consequential problem is the **5–7 s plateau**. Converting the model's 0.019–0.024 g at 5–7 s to pseudo-velocity gives only **about 19–21 cm/s**, which falls in JMA Class 2. Maruyama et al. report that PWSA and BKSI exceeded the Class 3 threshold (conventionally 50 cm/s) around 6 s. Separately, the BEE paper says the record exceeds the DPT design spectrum there. The target should have a distinct peak near 6–6.5 s of roughly 0.05 g or more, subject to checking Maruyama's actual Sva values. Because the model's as-built periods of 6.1 s and 7.65 s sit inside this band, the computed demand on the as-built model hinges on precisely the ordinates that look most underestimated. The 60 s duration should become 90–120 s with a long-period coda, or better, the models should be driven by the actual PWSA (surface) and BKSI records, requested from TMD or the BEE authors, rather than by synthetics alone.

The **periods** need the hardest scrutiny. Cracked-section modelling legitimately lengthens periods beyond ambient-vibration values. Combined with the thinned 25 cm core, the stiffness-poor flat slab and an eccentric core, it could plausibly reach 4–5 s. A 33-storey model at 6.1–7.65 s, however, is as flexible as Bangkok's 60-storey towers. Several checks are needed. First, confirm that the seismic mass uses dead load plus superimposed dead load appropriate to an unfinished shell, not full design live load. Second, confirm that the wall and slab cracked-stiffness modifiers are not compounded, and that the base fixity and diaphragm assumptions are deliberate. Third, confirm whether the 6.1 s mode is translational or a torsion-dominated coupled mode. Finally, run a sensitivity case tuned to about 2.7–3.3 s. That matters for interpretation. If the true period is about 3 s, the as-built building sat near the **2.8 s spectral peak**, and the proposed beam-slab scheme at 4.2–4.96 s would actually move it away from that peak. At the modelled 6.1 s, the resonance story shifts to the 6 s basin mode instead. The models should also reproduce the official failure location, **shear-wall failure at floors 1–4**, under 0.02 g-level input with the reduced concrete strength and short link-beam anchorage. That is the one hard validation target the investigation provides.

## Conclusion

The data reframe the SAO question. Bangkok's PGA was small and well constrained, so PGA calibration is the least informative part of any SAO model. The decisive inputs are the spectral ordinates at the building's own period and the number of cycles at that period. Those are exactly the quantities the user's current target spectrum and modal results get least right. The model's PGA, PGV and 1.3 s peak can all be correct while its demand at 6 s is too low by a factor of several. At the same time, its 6.1–7.65 s periods place the building in a band it probably did not occupy. The two errors interact, so a collapse outcome that matches the observed failure would not by itself validate either one.

The most valuable next step is to obtain the PWSA and BKSI time histories and run the as-built model at both its current period and a period of about 3 s. If collapse at floors 1–4 appears only in the second case, or only with the degraded concrete, the analysis will both support and sharpen the official, capacity-side finding. It can then say plainly whether the eccentric flat-slab core failed because it was weak, because it was tuned to the 2.8 s peak, or both.
