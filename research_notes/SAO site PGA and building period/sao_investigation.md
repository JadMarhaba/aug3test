# SAO building collapse (Chatuchak, 28 Mar 2025): official and forensic findings on ground motion, building dynamics and failure sequence

Method note: direct page fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by network policy in this session, so every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries and snippets of the cited pages, not from full-text reads. The numbers are attributed to the pages named, but they should be spot-checked against the originals before publication. I did not access any official full report (DPT committee report or SAO one-year report PDF).

## Q1. Which ground-motion record or intensity did the official investigation or forensic analyses apply to the SAO model, and what PGA/spectral values at the site did they cite?

### Takeaway
I found no public source that names the ground-motion record, PGA or spectrum that the official DPT-led committee (or its four university teams) applied to their SAO model. The published official findings (30 Jun 2025, repeated in the SAO one-year report of Mar 2026) talk only about forces "less than required by law" and do not give site motion values. The academic Bangkok-basin studies give city-wide values instead: PGA about 0.02 g, PGV about 10 cm/s, long-period amplification of about 10 times or more, and spectral peaks at 1-3 s and 5-7 s. None of them is a SAO-site record.

### Cited Findings
- In April 2025 DPT said it was building a "complex mathematical model" to test whether construction errors caused the collapse. It asked for 90 days, and four academic institutions would build independent simulations to be compared. No published results, input records or model properties were found. — [Fulcrum / search summary](https://fulcrum.sg/thai-state-audit-office-has-only-itself-to-blame-for-tragic-collapse-of-building-2/); [Bangkok Post, "SAO probe panel needs more time"](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2996504/collapse)
- The four institutions in the engineering probe were Chulalongkorn University, KMITL, Kasetsart University and KMUTT. — [Thai PBS Verify (EN)](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/verify/en/content/14616). Bangkok Post named three universities (Chula, Kasetsart, KMITL) alongside government agencies. This is a minor discrepancy. — [search summary of Bangkok Post via Thai-language search](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/3164113/audit-office-acknowledges-fatal-flaws-in-building)
- Ornthammarath et al. (Bull. Earthquake Eng., 2025), "Far-field ground motion characteristics of the Bangkok Basin... 2025 Mw 7.7 Mandalay earthquake: initial insights": "most strong motion sensors in Bangkok reported PGA and PGV ... of 0.02 g and 10 cm/s". They found significant long-period amplification in the deep basin, almost 10 times that at stiff-soil stations at a similar distance. — [Springer 10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- A figure caption describes the five Bangkok-basin stations as showing substantial spectral amplification at 1-3 s and 5-7 s. — [ResearchGate figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Map-showing-five-seismic-stations-located-in-the-Bangkok-basin-and-the-depth-to-the_fig3_396456422)
- Conflicting PGA: a ResearchGate paper on amplification and resonance in Bangkok soft soil cites TMD records with a maximum ground acceleration of about 0.08 g in Bangkok, about 4 times the 0.02 g above. — [ResearchGate 398628031](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area). Not resolved. It may be a different station, component or processing, and the paper's peer-review status is unclear.
- The same ResearchGate paper describes the Chatuchak area as having a 15-30 m water-saturated clay layer, Vs30 below 150 m/s and a dominant period of 1.8-2.5 s. It says this amplified motion up to about twice the theoretical value and attributes the Chatuchak collapse to resonance combined with poor construction. — [ResearchGate 398628031](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- J. Earthquake Eng. (2026), "Dynamic Characteristics of Tall Buildings in Bangkok Associated with the 28 March 2025 Mw 7.7 Teleseismic Earthquake": PGA was only about 0.02 g, but long-period spectral amplitudes were amplified more than 10 times. Several RC high-rises showed permanent period elongation of up to 40%. The abstract does not mention the SAO building. — [T&F 10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969)
- A 2026 J. Seismology paper, "Far-field ground motion characteristics of the 2025 Mw 7.7 Myanmar earthquake and implications for tall buildings", decomposes the Bangkok records (empirical mode decomposition) and tests their effect on tall-building response. Only the abstract was seen. — [Springer 10.1007/s10950-026-10440-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10950-026-10440-1)
- An HVSR study reports a dominant ground period of 0.7-2.5 s in central Bangkok, consistent with high-rise damage and strong-motion data from the 2025 event. — [ResearchGate 375890535](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/375890535_Preliminary_analysis_of_amplified_ground_motion_in_Bangkok_basin_using_HVSR_curves_from_recent_moderate_to_large_earthquakes)
- The 1.07 g peak acceleration in the Wikipedia earthquake article is a near-source value, not Bangkok. — [Wikipedia: 2025 Myanmar earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Myanmar_earthquake)
- Reported epicentral distance to Bangkok varies across sources (about 600, 700 and 1,000+ km). Thai media also used magnitudes of 7.4, 7.7 and 8.2 (TMD ML 8.2 vs USGS Mw 7.7). — [search summaries; Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Myanmar_earthquake); [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/350831)

### Inferences
- The official findings are framed as code-compliance and capacity deficiencies, not as a demand-exceeds-design argument. That is consistent with no site-specific record being published. The committee probably analysed the building against the legal design spectrum (DPT 1301/1302) and/or Bangkok basin records, but this is not confirmed.
- No free-field strong-motion station is reported at the SAO site itself. Any "site PGA" is extrapolated from Bangkok basin stations, at about 0.02 g PGA with large long-period spectral ordinates.

### Gaps
- I found no publication of the record(s), scaling or spectral ordinates used by DPT, the four universities, the Council of Engineers (COE) or DSI for the SAO model.
- I did not obtain station-by-station PGA or spectral values (Sa at 1-3 s) from Ornthammarath et al. (2025), because full text was not accessible.
- I could not reconcile the 0.02 g and 0.08 g PGA figures.

## Q2. Did any report give the SAO building's computed or estimated natural period, or note resonance with the soil or ground-motion period?

### Takeaway
I found no official or academic source that reports a computed or measured natural period for the SAO building. Resonance between Bangkok's soft-soil long-period motion and tall buildings was widely discussed by experts and by one ResearchGate paper. That paper says the soil's dominant period is 1.8-2.5 s and that buildings of about 15-25 storeys are affected. The official cause findings do not list resonance as a cause.

### Cited Findings
- Thai media expert commentary said most experts saw the building's natural frequency matching the long-period amplified shaking ("resonance"). This is commentary without SAO-specific numbers. — [Bangkok Biz News: soft soil amplification, high-rise resonance](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/environment/1175362); [Thairath interview: soft-soil basin, pancake collapse](https://www.thairath.co.th/scoop/interview/2850602); [The Matter: soft clay explainer](https://thematter.co/social/earth-quake-bangkok-clay/241382)
- The president of the Structural Engineers Association of Thailand said Bangkok's soft soil "may have also played a part", alongside concrete and reinforcement quality and "some irregularity in [the] structural system". — [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/60530-collapse-of-33-story-bangkok-tower-during-earthquake-blamed-on-substandard-construction)
- The ResearchGate paper ties ground-structure resonance (soil dominant period 1.8-2.5 s) to buildings of 15-25 storeys with deep piles, and attributes the Chatuchak collapse to resonance plus poor construction stability. — [ResearchGate 398628031](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- Older ambient-vibration work (13th WCEE) measured 50 Bangkok buildings 20-210 m tall. It found that buildings of 15-25 storeys have fundamental periods near the predominant period of amplified Bangkok soft-soil motion (about 1 s in that study). — [13WCEE paper 1264](https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/wcee/article/13_1264.pdf)
- The JEE 2026 paper reports permanent period elongation of up to 40% in instrumented Bangkok RC high-rises after the event. The SAO is not mentioned. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969)

### Inferences
- The SAO is a 30-storey building (about 33 levels including roof structures). With a typical T ≈ 0.02H (H ≈ 100-130 m), its fundamental period would be roughly 2-3 s. That falls in the 1-3 s band where Bangkok basin records show strong amplification. This is my own rule-of-thumb estimate, not a reported value.
- Because other Bangkok towers of similar height survived the same shaking, official findings attribute the collapse to deficiencies rather than to resonance alone.

### Gaps
- I found no reported computed period (ETABS/modal) or measured period for the SAO building from DPT, the universities, COE or DSI.
- I found no video-based sway amplitude or sway-period estimate for the SAO before collapse.

## Q3. What did investigators conclude about the failure sequence, and whether the shaking alone exceeded the design level (DPT 1301/1302)?

### Takeaway
The official finding (DPT chief engineer Pisut Sukhum's committee, announced by the PM on 30 Jun 2025 and repeated in the SAO one-year report of Mar 2026) was that collapse began on floors 1-4. Earthquake shear on the shear walls (lift and stair cores) caused them to fail. Four deficiencies were named: (1) failure location and mechanism, (2) shear-wall core concrete below the criterion, (3) non-compliant detailed drawings (the building could resist less force than legally required), and (4) link-beam rebar embedment shorter than legally required. The official framing is that a building designed and built to the law would have been stronger. They did not conclude that the shaking alone exceeded the code design level. Thai PBS Verify summarises the outcome as the collapse "not caused by the earthquake alone".

### Cited Findings
- Official sequence: "the collapse began in the lower part of the building (floors 1-4) due to earthquake-induced shear acting on the shear walls (lift walls and stair walls) until failure." — [ThaiPublica, 30 Jun 2025](https://thaipublica.org/2025/06/prime-minister-reveals-audit-office-collapse-report/); [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/353750); [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40051947)
- The four causes repeated in the SAO one-year report (Mar 2026): floors 1-4 shear failure; shear-wall core sample average below criterion; drawings not compliant with law, "resulting in the building being able to withstand less force than required by law"; and link-beam to shear-wall rebar shorter than legally required, creating weak joints. — [Nation, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.nationthailand.com/blogs/news/general/40064045); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/03/20/thai-audit-office-releases-report-on-building-collapse-cites-four-main-causes); [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/society/2921681); [Thai PBS 1-year engineering summary](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/503912)
- ThaiPublica says the drawings did not conform to the building-control law in force (Ministerial Regulation No. 6 B.E. 2527 and DPT standards 1301/1302), and that "if the detailed drawings had complied with the law, the building (shear walls) would have been stronger". — [ThaiPublica](https://thaipublica.org/2025/06/prime-minister-reveals-audit-office-collapse-report/)
- Thai PBS Verify says the design details referenced ACI 318-08 and DPT 1302-52, which did not comply with the building regulations legally in force. It also says link-beam/shear-wall anchorage was shorter than required, and that the probe concluded the collapse was not caused by the earthquake alone. — [Thai PBS Verify (EN)](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/verify/en/content/14616)
- A government-appointed engineering team said the design was "not in line" with the codes under the 2007 ministerial regulation, and that the codes themselves are valid for earthquakes. — [Bangkok Tribune](https://bkktribune.com/design-of-collapsed-under-construction-sao-building-not-in-line-with-regulation/)
- Panel interim focus (Apr-May 2025): an asymmetrical design (core offset to the rear), with the panel chair invoking cyclic torsion. — [Bangkok Tribune](https://bkktribune.com/govt-appointed-panel-focuses-its-probe-on-design-of-collapsed-sao-building/)
- Police/DSI account: the elevator shaft wall was near the rear, shifting the torsional centre. The shaft wall and base columns failed nearly at the same time, and the building collapsed vertically. — [The Civil Engineer](https://www.thecivilengineer.org/news/bangkok-30-storey-audit-office-collapse-attributed-to-construction-defects)
- Fulcrum reports that some experts thought the collapse started at the top of the core wall. This conflicts with the official floors 1-4 finding. — [Fulcrum](https://fulcrum.sg/thai-state-audit-office-has-only-itself-to-blame-for-tragic-collapse-of-building-2/)
- The SAO's April 2025 position was that the building was quake-proof (designed to code). This was later contradicted by the official findings. — [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40048611)
- Other Bangkok buildings: the SAO was the only building to collapse. Bangkok Tribune reported that at least three other Bangkok buildings had core walls severely damaged in a similar pattern without collapsing. — [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40048611); [ENR/Bangkok Tribune via search](https://bkktribune.com/design-of-collapsed-under-construction-sao-building-not-in-line-with-regulation/)
- Structural system: experts and ENR describe a flat-slab (post-tensioned) floor system with a concrete core-wall lateral system. Video-based commentary (Art of Structures) hypothesises that the core lost gravity capacity at its base, followed by slab punching. These are not official findings. — [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/60530-collapse-of-33-story-bangkok-tower-during-earthquake-blamed-on-substandard-construction); [Art of Structures](https://artofstructures.com/2025/04/26/reasons-behind-bangkok-high-rise-collapse-during-earthquake/)

### Inferences
- The official logic is capacity-side, not demand-side: code-compliant detailing and specified concrete would have given more shear-wall capacity. That implies, without stating it numerically, that the Bangkok shaking was within what a code-compliant building should survive.
- The "1302-52 vs. law in force" point suggests the designers used an older seismic standard rather than the legally applicable one. The applicable DPT 1301/1302-61 (2018) and the 2021 Interior Ministry regulation govern Bangkok's soft-soil spectrum.

### Gaps
- I found no numerical demand/capacity ratio, base-shear comparison or design-spectrum ordinate comparison published by the committee.
- I could not confirm from an official text whether the committee explicitly said the shaking was below the DPT 1301/1302-61 design spectrum at the SAO's period.

## Q4. Exact numbers: concrete strength (tested vs required), wall thickness, embedment, collapse duration

### Takeaway
Officially, the shear-wall core average was "below criterion", with no numbers in the official summaries. Numbers circulated by Choolert Chitjuajun, vice-president of the Structural Engineers Association, citing the Council of Engineers. Under the 500 ksc specification (the collapsed tower), 37.8% of wall samples were below 75% of the specification (<375 ksc). Under the 350 ksc specification (a non-collapsed building in the project), 36.8% or 60% of samples (sources differ) were below <262.5 ksc. The core wall thickness was reduced from 30 cm to 25 cm by a design change. No embedment length numbers were found. The video-based collapse duration is about 8 s (Worsak Kanok-Nukulchai); another analysis cites about 5.5 s of fall.

### Cited Findings
- Concrete (COE data via SEAT vice-president Choolert): specs of 500 ksc (collapsed tower) and 350 ksc. Walls "failed almost entirely". For 500 ksc, wall samples below 75% of spec (<375 ksc) made up 37.8%, and both walls and columns failed. — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5259494); [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/4879250/); [Thaiger (TH)](https://thethaiger.com/th/news/1424292/)
- Discrepancy for the 350 ksc group: Matichon gives 60% of wall samples below 262.5 ksc. Daily News gives 36.8% below 262.5 ksc and says it refers to the non-collapsed building. Thaiger says "only walls failed", with 60% below the minimum. — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5259494); [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/4879250/); [Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/th/news/1424292/)
- Sampling: more than 300 structural pieces were collected, and 65 were selected for testing (37 lift-shaft wall pieces and 28 concrete columns). This was per SEAT via the Thai search summary, and the source page was not directly identified. — [search summary citing Thai-language reports](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5259494)
- An SAO clarification references DPT 1231-2562, under which 28-day strength must be at least 325 ksc or as specified in the drawings. The context is the inspection-criterion explanation, not a test result. — [Thailand Plus](https://www.thailandplus.tv/archives/468170)
- I found no "280 ksc" figure tied to the SAO in the sources searched.
- Official wording on materials is inconsistent. The PM's June 2025 statement said the steel and general concrete met standards, but the project's concrete and its construction were sub-standard. Wikipedia reports that materials met standards. Bangkok Biz News and the SAO 2026 report say core concrete fell below criterion. Earlier (Apr 2025), Industry Ministry/TISI tests said 32 mm bars failed strength and 20 mm bars failed the mass-per-metre requirement. — [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/thai-pm-reveals-probe-results-on-collapsed-state-audit-office-building-design-and-construction-violated-the-law-507654); [Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/news/news-update/1214121); [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/60530-collapse-of-33-story-bangkok-tower-during-earthquake-blamed-on-substandard-construction); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_Thailand_State_Audit_Office_building)
- Wall thickness: the core/lift-shaft walls were reduced from 30 cm to 25 cm in a design change, offset by added reinforcement. It was one of nine design changes. The SAO said the change complied with standards. — [Thailand Construction](https://thailand-construction.com/reduced-thickness-of-elevator-shaft-walls-now-in-focus-on-bangkoks-collapsed-state-audit-office-building/); [Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/news/bangkok/audit-office-blames-thin-elevator-shafts-for-bangkok-collapse); [Fulcrum](https://fulcrum.sg/thai-state-audit-office-has-only-itself-to-blame-for-tragic-collapse-of-building-2/)
- Justice Minister Tawee Sodsong said such walls "should typically be about 60 cm" but the SAO's were 25 cm. This is a political statement, not an engineering finding. — [Thai PBS World](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/news/moibnj)
- Embedment: link-beam to shear-wall bar embedment/anchorage was "shorter than legally required". No numerical lengths were found in any source. — [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/blogs/news/general/40064045); [Thai PBS Verify](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/verify/en/content/14616)
- Collapse duration: Prof. Emeritus Worsak Kanok-Nukulchai (former AIT president) analysed social-media video in slow motion. He described an "8 critical seconds" pancake collapse that started at the rear-offset lift core, with torsion caused by the core's eccentricity, and with 45° diagonal fractures in columns consistent with twisting. The first visible failure was two columns. He called it a world record. Posted around 15 Apr 2025. — [Isranews](https://www.isranews.org/article/isranews-article/137265-wrs-4.html); [PPTV](https://www.pptvhd36.com/news/%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A1/246856); [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/4607638/); [Bangkok Tribune EN](https://bkktribune.com/8-critical-seconds-how-the-30-storey-building-collapsed-into-a-layer-cake/); [Posttoday](https://www.posttoday.com/smart-city/723163)
- An alternative video analysis (YouTube) says the lower lift core twisted and broke first and the building fell in about 5.5 s. It is non-authoritative. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HJamuIkXlgQ)
- Kasetsart's Assoc. Prof. Amorn Pimanmas also identified the lift core at the rear as the starting point. — [The Standard](https://thestandard.co/sao-building-collapse-investigation/); [Bangkok Tribune](https://bkktribune.com/govt-appointed-panel-focuses-its-probe-on-design-of-collapsed-sao-building/)
- Legal status: prosecutors indicted 23 suspects (companies and individuals). The case is before the court and no conviction had been reported as of Mar 2026. The SAO will not rebuild on the site. — [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40060405); [Nation, one year on](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40064310); [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/3222849/state-audit-office-rules-out-headquarters-rebuild-at-collapse-site)

### Inferences
- The 75%-of-specification thresholds (375/262.5 ksc) are the usual core-test acceptance criterion (as in ACI 318: average ≥ 85% of f'c and no single core < 75%). The reported shares are fractions of samples below the individual-core limit, not mean strengths.
- The "8 s" figure is an unofficial video-based estimate. Worsak himself declined further investigation and deferred to the state committee. The official sequence (floors 1-4 shear-wall failure) is broadly consistent with his core-first account, but the committee emphasised shear and the lower storeys rather than torsion.

### Gaps
- I found no mean or individual measured core strengths (ksc) from the official committee.
- I found no numerical embedment lengths (provided vs required).
- I found no official measurement of sway amplitude, oscillation count or time from shaking onset to collapse; only video-based estimates of collapse duration.
- No GEER/EERI reconnaissance report with SAO-specific dynamic data surfaced in the searches.
