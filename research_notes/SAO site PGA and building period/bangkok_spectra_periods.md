## Q1. Recorded spectral values, amplified period bands, dominant period, JMA long-period class, and amplification

### Takeaway
Bangkok's five TMD basin stations recorded small peaks: "most" reported PGA ≈ 0.02 g and PGV ≈ 10 cm/s, the largest ever recorded in the city. The response spectra show strong long-period amplification in two bands, **1–3 s and 5–7 s**. That amplification is about **10×** the level at stiff-soil stations at similar distance, and "more than ten times" in the long-period range according to a 2026 tall-building study. Maruyama et al. (TSER2025) classify the motion as **JMA long-period ground motion Class 3** with dominant periods **around 6 s**. I could not find numeric Sa/Sv/Sd values at specific periods.

### Cited Findings
- **Paper identity.** Ornthammarath, Warnitchai, Maruyama, Pornsopin & Yamazaki, "Far-field ground motion characteristics of the Bangkok Basin, Thailand, in the 2025 Mw 7.7 Mandalay earthquake: initial insights," *Bull. Earthquake Eng.* 23(15):6577–6602 (2025), doi 10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7. A 2026 erratum (doi 10.1007/s10518-026-02368-1) corrects a typesetting error in the equation on p. 10. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7); [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/handle/123456789/112714)
- **Network.** 27 accelerometers in northern and western Thailand recorded the mainshock, five of them in the Bangkok basin. The TMD network recorded the event at 23 stations at source-to-site distances of 200–600 km. — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- **Peak values.** "Most strong motion sensors in Bangkok reported PGA and PGV from this event of 0.02 g and 10 cm/s, respectively, which are the largest ground motion ever recorded in Thailand's capital city." — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
  - **Conflict:** a separate paper says TMD recorded a maximum ground acceleration of about **0.08 g** in Bangkok, at MMI IV–V. It is unclear whether this is another station, a different measure, or an error. — [ResearchGate preprint, "Seismic amplification and resonance effects of the 2025 Myanmar earthquake in Bangkok's soft-soil area"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- **Amplified bands.** "All recorded response spectra in Bangkok show long period soil amplification within the period range of 1–3 s and 5–7 s." — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- **Size of amplification relative to stiff soil.** "The amplification of long period ground motion for the five stations in Bangkok is evident, which is almost 10 times for stiff soil stations located at a similar distance." — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- **Relative to GMMs.** Compared with NGA-West2 GMMs, the data show positive residuals at long distances, i.e. slower attenuation than the models predict. "For SA at the period of 1.0 s, the obvious underestimation of all considered GMMs compared to the recorded ground motion is still noticeable for the distance between 200 and 600 km." At 6.0 s the attenuation rate is slower than the GMMs predict. — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- **Amplification ">10×".** "Although the peak ground acceleration was only about 0.02 g, spectral amplitudes in the long-period range were amplified by more than ten times, causing significant vibration in tall buildings." The same study reports permanent period elongation of up to 40% in several RC high-rises. — ["Dynamic Characteristics of Tall Buildings in Bangkok Associated with the 28 March 2025 Mw 7.7 Teleseismic Earthquake," J. Earthquake Eng. (2026), doi 10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13632469.2026.2676969)
- **JMA class and 6 s period.** Maruyama, Yamazaki, Ornthammarath & Warnitchai, "Long-Period Ground Motion in Bangkok from the 2025 Mandalay Earthquake: JMA Scale Application" (TSER2025), apply the JMA long-period ground motion intensity scale and report **Class 3** motions with dominant periods **around 6 s**. Only the abstract-level statement was retrievable. I could not read the Sva values (cm/s), the per-station classes, or the per-component results. — [EARTH Digital Library, TSER2025 item 32](https://earth.nrct.go.th/EarthLib/item.php?id=32)
- **Shorter dominant periods from a soft-clay resonance study.** The paper reports "a 15–30 m thick water-saturated clay layer with Vs30 below 150 m/s and a dominant period of 1.8–2.5 seconds amplified ground motion up to twice the theoretical value." It attributes the Chatuchak (SAO) collapse to resonance combined with poor construction, and links the resonance to 15–25 storey buildings. This is a single preprint-level study, and its collapse attribution is not settled. — [ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- **Industry estimate (secondary source).** The 2025 motions in Bangkok were in the "0.8 to 3.4 second period range, which corresponds to the natural resonant frequencies of buildings between 13 and 55 stories." — [WTW insight, Aug 2025](https://www.wtwco.com/en-sg/insights/2025/08/long-waves-and-tall-buildings)
- **Range quoted in one summary.** "The Bangkok basin shows broad, low-frequency amplification (T0≈4–7 s), a direct result of deep soft Quaternary sediments." The excerpt did not clearly show which paper this came from; it appeared alongside the TMD/BEE-related results. — [search excerpt associated with Springer BEE / ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- **Not Bangkok.** A *J. Seismology* paper (doi 10.1007/s10950-026-10440-1) on far-field 2025 motions and tall buildings reports that a representative far-field record at **271 km** epicentral distance exceeds a design spectrum "for intensity 8". It also reports that removing the dominant component near the structure's period cuts tall-building response by up to 28.76%. 271 km is not Bangkok, which is roughly 1,000 km from the epicentre, so this does not apply to Bangkok. — [Springer J. Seismology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10950-026-10440-1)

### Inferences
- The two bands match the two known site periods. **1–3 s** is the shallow soft-clay mode (about 1 s), lengthened by nonlinearity or modestly deeper soft deposits. **5–7 s** is the deep basin mode (500–800 m of sediment), and the ~6 s JMA dominant period is that deep-basin resonance.
- JMA Class 3 means Sva(h=5%) in the 50–100 cm/s range at some period between 1.6 and 7.8 s. That range is my own knowledge of the JMA scale definition, not from a retrieved source. If it applies, Bangkok long-period Sva peaked at roughly 50–100 cm/s, likely near 6 s.
- An Sva of 50–100 cm/s at T = 6 s converts to pseudo-Sa ≈ 2π/T × Sv ≈ 0.05–0.10 m/s² ≈ **0.005–0.011 g**. At T ≈ 2 s the same Sv would give about 0.016–0.032 g. These are back-of-envelope inferences only and should not be cited as recorded values. They are consistent with a 0.02 g PGA and with the design spectrum exceeding records below 5 s.

### Gaps
- No numeric Sa, Sv or Sd values at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 10 s for any Bangkok station could be retrieved. They exist in figures of the BEE paper, the TSER2025 Maruyama abstract, and Warnitchai's 22 May 2025 BKNIX slide deck ("The Bangkok Earthquake and What we learned from it"), but none of those could be fetched.
- Station codes (e.g., TMDA, TMDB, CHBT) and their locations relative to Chatuchak were not retrievable. The nearest Bangkok station to the SAO site is unknown.
- Per-station JMA class and Sva values are unknown.

## Q2. Strong-motion (significant) duration of the Bangkok records

### Takeaway
I found **no published D5-95 or other Arias-based significant duration for the 2025 Bangkok records**. The only figure is TMD's macroseismic statement that shaking lasted "more than one minute". Pre-2025 basin records show significant duration about **1.5×** that at nearby rock or stiff sites, because of basin-generated surface waves.

### Cited Findings
- "According to a report by the Thai Meteorological Department (TMD), the intensity of the tremor in the city reached IV-V on the Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) scale with a vibration duration of more than one minute." — [ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- The same paper says soft layers with Vs30 < 150 m/s strengthen amplitude and extend the shaking, "especially for high-rise buildings with natural periods of 1.5–2.5 seconds". It also notes that Ornthammarath et al. (2025) "did not evaluate rupture-to-site azimuthal dependence or duration characteristics", which confirms that the BEE paper gives no duration metrics. — [ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- **Pre-2025 events, TMDA station vs SRDT/PRAC rock or stiff sites.** "The significant duration in the Arias Intensity at the TMDA station is about 1.5 times greater than those recorded at SRDT and PRAC." This is attributed to locally induced long-period surface waves in the basin. After the surface wave arrives, low-frequency content (1–0.3 Hz, i.e., 1–3.3 s) dominates for 3–4 min. Basin sediments lengthen duration even for weak shaking. — [Ornthammarath et al. 2023, Geoenvironmental Disasters 10:28, doi 10.1186/s40677-023-00259-0](https://geoenvironmental-disasters.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40677-023-00259-0)
- The ~80 s figure for the 2025 event is the **rupture** duration of the ~450–500 km Sagaing rupture, not ground-motion duration in Bangkok. — [Wikipedia, 2025 Myanmar earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Myanmar_earthquake)

### Inferences
- Three things together suggest Bangkok's 2025 D5-95 was likely well over 60 s, plausibly 1–3 min. They are an ~80 s rupture duration, ~1,000 km of travel distance (body to surface-wave dispersion), and a ~1.5× basin lengthening factor. This is an inference and is unverified. Anyone generating synthetic "Bangkok-2025-like" motions should treat duration as a sensitivity parameter, not a known value.

### Gaps
- No instrumental D5-75 or D5-95 for the 2025 Bangkok records. The 2026 JEE tall-building paper and the 2026 *J. Seismology* far-field paper might report one, but only their abstracts were accessible.

## Q3. Measured site periods of Bangkok soil (shallow soft clay vs deep basin), especially near Chatuchak

### Takeaway
Bangkok has **two site periods**:
- **Shallow soft-clay mode:** about **0.7–1.2 s**. Microtremor H/V gives 0.8–1.2 s in the thick-clay south and 0.68–0.86 s at five sites; northern areas are shorter.
- **Deep basin mode:** about **2–5 s** from a 115-site H/V study. Earthquake-record H/V peaks at **about 5 s**, and the 2025 spectra show 5–7 s. Bedrock lies 300–800 m deep.

I found no site-specific period for the Chatuchak/SAO site. The soft clay thins northward, so its shallow mode is probably at or below the ~1 s central-city value.

### Cited Findings
- **Tuladhar et al. 2004** (EESD 33, doi 10.1002/eqe.345) measured H/V at >150 sites. Areas near the Gulf of Thailand with thick soft clay have "long natural periods ranging from 0.8s to 1.2s". Areas outside the lower central plain are under 0.4 s. Another summary gives the overall range as 0.4–1.2 s. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/eqe.345); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227669148_Seismic_microzonation_of_the_greater_Bangkok_area_using_microtremor_observations)
- Soft clay is about 15–20 m thick in central Bangkok, increasing gradually toward the Gulf and "decreasing more rapidly towards the north". — [Tuladhar et al. 2004 via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227669148_Seismic_microzonation_of_the_greater_Bangkok_area_using_microtremor_observations)
- **Five-site HVSR plus array study (Frontiers in Built Environment 2021).** The predominant period is "within 0.68–0.86 s". The top clay has Vs of 82–120 m/s to depths of 11–14.3 m. "HVSR of seismic waves recorded inside the Bangkok basin shows a low-frequency peak at around 5 s." — [Frontiers Built Env. 2021, doi 10.3389/fbuil.2021.651902](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2021.651902/full)
- **Pananont, Naksawee, Hayashi, Furlong, Pornsopin & Muangnoi** (SRL 96(6):3409–3420, Nov 2025) used 115 sites. "Ground response in most areas has 2 predominant periods, 0.8–1.2 s and 2–5 s." They find VS < 500 m/s at depths < 200 m, and bedrock (VS 1000–1500 m/s) at 300–500 m. — [Penn State Pure record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/shear-wave-velocity-structure-and-site-response-analysis-of-the-b/); [TSER2025 extended abstract](https://earth.nrct.go.th/EarthLib/item.php?id=42)
- **Poovarodom & Jirasakjamroonsri** (2ECCES) ran microtremor SPAC arrays at 16 sites and found bedrock depth "varies from 550 m to 800 m". Their design spectra show long-period effects "for the period up to 3 seconds". — [EAEE 2ECCES paper 1482](https://www.eaee.org/Media/Default/2ECCES/2ecces_eaee/1482.pdf)
- **Poovarodom & Plalinyot** (J. Earthquake Eng. 17(2):209–226, 2013) carried out site characterization of greater Bangkok by microtremor observations. Period values were not retrievable. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13632469.2012.707346)
- **Warnitchai 2004 (13WCEE paper 744).** Soft deposits amplify motions "about 3 to 4 times". Amplified motions are "narrowband random motions with a long predominant period of about 1 second", and 15–25 storey buildings are near resonance. — [13WCEE 744](https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/wcee/article/13_744.pdf)
- **SAO site location.** Kamphaeng Phet 2 Road, Chatuchak District (northern Bangkok). — [Wikipedia, Collapse of Thailand State Audit Office building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_Thailand_State_Audit_Office_building)

### Inferences
- For Chatuchak in northern Bangkok, the evidence points to a shallow soft-clay period of about **0.6–1.0 s**: the city-wide 0.68–1.2 s range, plus clay thinning northward. The deep-basin mode is about **4–7 s**: the 2–5 s H/V band, the ~5 s earthquake H/V peak, and the 5–7 s amplified band and ~6 s dominant period in 2025. The ~6 s JMA result is consistent with a 300–800 m deep sedimentary column.
- **Disagreements to flag.**
  - Microtremor H/V gives ~0.7–1.2 s, but a 2025 preprint gives 1.8–2.5 s for 15–30 m clay. That is long for 15–30 m of Vs ≈ 100 m/s clay, where T = 4H/Vs ≈ 0.6–1.2 s. The preprint value may reflect nonlinear softening or a combined shallow and intermediate mode.
  - Deep-mode estimates range from 2–5 s (H/V) to ~6 s (2025 spectra, JMA).

### Gaps
- No microtremor or H/V measurement specific to Chatuchak, Bang Sue or the SAO site was found.
- Tuladhar's map values for northern Bangkok could not be read from figures.

## Q4. Comparison with the Thai design spectrum (DPT 1301/1302-61, Bangkok zones)

### Takeaway
The recorded Bangkok spectra are **below the DPT 1301/1302 design spectrum for 0–5 s** (and specifically 1–3 s) and **exceed it above about 5 s**. The Bangkok zone 5 spectrum used as reference has Sa(1 s) = **0.20 g (DBE)** and **0.30 g (MCE)**. The 2018 revision raised Bangkok Sa at 1 s by about 25% and lowered it at 6 s by about 28% relative to DPT 1302-52 (2009). That 6 s reduction falls in exactly the band the 2025 records exceeded.

### Cited Findings
- "The DPT1301/1302 design spectrum is greater than that of recorded spectral ordinates between 0 and 5 s. However, the recorded spectrum does exceed the design spectrum for periods above 5 s." Another excerpt specifies that the design spectrum exceeds records "between 1 and 3 s, which corresponds to the fundamental period of 20–50 storey buildings". — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- "The SA at the period of 1 s of DPT 1301/1302 spectra are 0.20 and 0.30 g for Design Basis Earthquake (DBE) and Maximum Considered Earthquake (MCE) levels, respectively". This is for the Bangkok zone 5 reference spectrum used in the BEE paper. — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- The Bangkok design spectrum has "high spectral acceleration centered at about 1 s, and a very low acceleration reduction between 1 s and around 2.0 s". It accounts for basin amplification through 1-D ground response analysis from the surface to 100 m depth. — [Springer BEE excerpt](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- **Poovarodom (DPT 1301/1302-61, 2018 revision).**
  - The revision introduced new design spectra for Bangkok and its vicinity that consider deep-basin effects, subdividing the area into **10 zones**.
  - "The new SA of Bangkok is about 25% higher at period of 1.0 second but it is lower for 28% at long period of 6.0 second" compared with 2009.
  - The MCE is derived from CMS at 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 s for a 2,475-year return period.
  - The tall-building provisions target RC structures taller than 60 m, and other structure types use a 2.5%-damped spectrum.
  — [Poovarodom, "A New Earthquake Resistant Design Standard for Buildings in Thailand" (SPU dspace)](https://dspace.spu.ac.th/bitstream/123456789/5894/1/ACEE0232_Poovarodom_A%20New%20Earthquake%20Resistant%20Design%20Standard%20for%20Buildings%20in%20Thailand.pdf)
- Warnitchai's 22 May 2025 slides state that the new Bangkok-area design spectra "have already been included in DPT1301/1302-61". — [Warnitchai, "The Bangkok Earthquake and What we learned from it" (BKNIX Peering Forum)](https://peeringforum.bknix.co.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/1.0-Bangkok-EQ-BKNIK-PF-22May2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- With Sa_DBE(1 s) = 0.20 g and the inferred recorded Sa of a few hundredths of a g at 1–3 s, the 2025 motion was probably **on the order of 10–20% of the DBE spectrum** at 1–3 s. This is inferred from PGA ≈ 0.02 g and a design spectrum "greater" over 0–5 s, not read from a figure. Even an elastically designed, code-compliant building in the 1–3 s range should not have been loaded near its design level by the 2025 event. This supports attributing the SAO collapse mainly to structural deficiencies rather than demand above design.
- The "above 5 s" exceedance matters mainly for very flexible systems such as super-tall towers, long-span bridges and liquid sloshing. It does not matter much for a ~30-storey RC core-wall building, whose fundamental period is about 2–4 s.

### Gaps
- No zone 1–10 Sa table was retrieved, so the **zone covering Chatuchak was not confirmed**. Zone 5 is only the BEE paper's reference zone.
- No numeric ratio of recorded to design Sa at specific periods. The crossover is stated only as "above 5 s".
- Sa values of the DPT spectrum at 2, 4 and 6 s were not retrieved. The official DPT 1301/1302-61 document (Thai) is needed.
