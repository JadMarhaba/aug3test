## Q1. What PGA/PGV did each Bangkok-area accelerometer record?

### Takeaway
Five TMD free-field strong-motion stations in the Bangkok basin recorded the event: PTNA, PWSA, KMUA, BKKA, plus the 30 m borehole station BKSI. The peer-reviewed summary is PGA ≈ 0.02 g (≈20 cm/s²) and PGV ≈ 10 cm/s at "most" Bangkok sensors. PWSA, the central and deepest-basin station, has the best-documented record: about 23.7 / 20.5 / 8.8 cm/s² (EW/NS/UD) and PGV about 13.9 / 10.8 / 4.4 cm/s. One presentation table gives BKKA only 0.004 g, which is unresolved. I found no published station-level records from Chulalongkorn, Kasetsart, AIT, DMR or building-mounted instruments.

### Inferences
- All five Bangkok stations are TMD stations. "Mahidol / AIT / Chiba" are the analysts, not separate operators. I found no independent free-field records from Chula, Kasetsart, AIT, DMR or the Earthquake Research Center of Thailand in the public literature.
- In SI units, the "0.02 g" headline is about 15–25 cm/s² given two-decimal rounding. The best-resolved horizontal value is PWSA's peak component, about 23–24 cm/s² (0.023–0.024 g), and its geometric-mean horizontal is about 22 cm/s².
- BKSI is a 30 m-deep borehole sensor, so it sits below most of the soft clay and stiff clay layers. Its 0.02 g is not a surface free-field value, and surface motion directly above may differ.

## Q2. Is there a source reporting ~0.08 g in Bangkok (Ismail et al., E3S Web of Conf., ICDMM 2025)?

### Takeaway
Yes. The ResearchGate/E3S paper "Seismic amplification and resonance effects of the 2025 Myanmar earthquake in Bangkok's soft-soil area" (E3S Web of Conferences, ICDMM 2025, article 09003) states that TMD recorded "a maximum ground acceleration of about 0.08 g" with MMI IV–V in the Bangkok area. However, the same paper also cites "a TMD report of roughly 0.04–0.06 g" and frames 0.08 g as what "field observations and numerical simulations" suggest for soft soil. No TMD instrumental record supports 0.08 g: TMD's own five Bangkok sensors, as analysed by Ornthammarath et al., peaked at about 0.02 g. The 0.08 g figure should be treated as unsupported, about 3–4 times the recorded values.

### Cited Findings
- Extract wording: "the Thai Meteorological Department (TMD) recorded a maximum ground acceleration of about 0.08 g and an IV–V shock intensity on the Modified Mercalli (MMI) scale in the Bangkok area." — [ResearchGate, Ismail et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area); [E3S PDF](https://www.e3s-conferences.org/articles/e3sconf/pdf/2025/77/e3sconf_icdmm2025_09003.pdf)
- The same paper, per extract, whose text is "garbled in places":
  - It cites a TMD report of about 0.04–0.06 g.
  - It says "field observations and numerical simulations show that the actual acceleration can reach 0.08 g in soft soil areas".
  - Another extract framed this as 0.08 g "compared with the 0.04–0.06 g typical of design expectations".
  - So it is ambiguous whether 0.04–0.06 g is a "TMD report" or a design value.
  - [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- Abstract content of the paper:
  - A 15–30 m water-saturated clay layer, Vs30 < 150 m/s, dominant period 1.8–2.5 s, amplified motion "up to twice the theoretical value".
  - It attributes the Chatuchak (SAO) collapse to resonance plus poor construction stability.
  - It states the epicentral distance as about 600 km.
  - [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- The recorded TMD values contradict it: most Bangkok sensors recorded 0.02 g. — [Springer BEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7). The station table gives a maximum of 0.02 g. — [TIEMS slides](https://tiems.info/images/pdf/seismic-hazard.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 0.08 g figure is not traceable to any TMD instrumental record in the sources found. It is either a misreading, for example of a different unit, spectral ordinate or provincial station, or a simulation or assumed value. It should not be used as the SAO site PGA.
- The "~0.08 g" could plausibly be a spectral acceleration near the basin's 1–3 s periods, or a figure from a provincial station closer to the source. This is speculation; no source confirms it.

### Gaps
- I could not open the E3S PDF to give the verbatim sentence with its citation and reference number, or to see which TMD document it cites. Verify this directly.
- The authors' full list and affiliations were not confirmed beyond "Ismail et al.".

## Q3. USGS ShakeMap and other official estimates for Bangkok/Chatuchak

### Takeaway
No Bangkok-specific USGS ShakeMap PGA/PGV/MMI value could be retrieved, because the USGS event pages and grid were blocked. Secondary sources put USGS-type intensity in Bangkok at about MMI V and PGA at about 1–2 %g. Analysts warn that ShakeMap under-represents basin amplification and long-period motion in Bangkok.

### Cited Findings
- USGS event us7000pn9s ShakeMap products (intensity, PGA, PGV, grids; latest version cited in an extract as v23) exist. The "stations" list shown in the extract held only DYFI entries near Mandalay. — [USGS ShakeMap intensity](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us7000pn9s/shakemap/intensity); [USGS stations](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us7000pn9s/shakemap/stations)
- "MMI V (Moderate) shaking was recorded as far as Bangkok and Chiang Mai". — [ScienceDirect review, "In the wake of the March 28, 2025 Myanmar earthquake"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950576325000017)
- A USGS-based summary cited Bangkok-area PGA of "1–2% g". — search extract, originating document not identified, likely the [Guy Carpenter report](https://www.guycarp.com/content/dam/guycarp-rebrand/insights-images/2025/04/04_03_2025_MM_EQ_Report_20250403_v2.pdf). Unverified.
- Guy Carpenter on ShakeMap limits:
  - Shake maps "may not adequately capture the effects of basin amplification and supershear events and so the intensity shown may be underestimated for areas such as Bangkok".
  - "MMI in general is a poor representation of long-period ground motion".
  - [Guy Carpenter](https://www.guycarp.com/content/dam/guycarp-rebrand/insights-images/2025/04/04_03_2025_MM_EQ_Report_20250403_v2.pdf)
- Ismail et al. attribute MMI IV–V in Bangkok to TMD. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398628031_Seismic_amplification_and_resonance_effects_of_the_2025_Myanmar_earthquake_in_Bangkok's_soft-soil_area)
- TMD's initial magnitude was 8.2, versus 7.7 from USGS. — [Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/2025/03/28/strong-earthquake-rocks-bangkok-prompting-evacuations-from-swaying-buildings/)
- The Bangkok design spectrum in the current standard peaks near 1 s, with little reduction from 1 to about 2 s. — [Springer BEE, via extract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- One secondary source says DPT 1302-52 was "based on underestimated PGA values of 0.1–0.15 g". — search extract, source not identified; flag.
- Rock-site hazard: PGA 0.03 g at 475 yr and 0.07 g at 2475 yr. — cited in [Ornthammarath et al. 2023](https://geoenvironmental-disasters.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40677-023-00259-0)

### Inferences
- MMI V corresponds roughly to PGA of 4–9 %g under common GMICEs, while the actual records are about 2 %g. This mismatch is consistent with Bangkok intensity being driven by long-period, long-duration motion rather than PGA.

### Gaps
- The USGS ShakeMap grid value at 13.80 N, 100.55 E (PGA, PGV, MMI, Vs30 used) was not retrieved, because earthquake.usgs.gov was blocked. It can be obtained from the grid.xml of us7000pn9s.
- No TMD or DPT official intensity report specific to Chatuchak was found.

## Q4. Inside vs outside the soft-clay basin; which value best represents the SAO site

### Takeaway
Inside the basin, PGA was about 20 mg (0.02 g); outside, about 3–6 mg. PGA amplification was about 3 times, and PGV amplification about 4–5 times in the horizontal. No station is co-located with the SAO site at about 13.80 N, 100.55 E. The nearest station with known coordinates is BKSI, about 8.3 km SSE, but it is a 30 m borehole. The best-documented surface record is PWSA, central Bangkok and deepest basin. The best representative value for the SAO site is therefore **PGA ≈ 0.02 g (≈20–24 cm/s², PWSA peak horizontal 23.7 cm/s²) and PGV ≈ 10–14 cm/s**, with long-period content peaking around 6 s and secondary peaks at about 1.6 s and 2.8 s.

### Cited Findings
- Warnitchai, head of the Earthquake Research Center of Thailand (Nation, reprinted by ANN and The Star, April 2026): "Peak ground acceleration outside the soft-soil basin may reach only 3–6 mg … but can increase to around 20 mg once seismic waves enter the basin." Amplified periods are around 1.6 s, 2.8 s and 6.3 s, and shaking lasted up to about 2 minutes. — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/bangkok/40064540); [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/bangkoks-soft-soil-basin-could-amplify-earthquake-shaking-by-3-6-times/); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/04/03/bangkoks-soft-soil-basin-could-amplify-earthquake-shaking-by-three-to-six-times)
- BEE paper:
  - "recorded PGA values for stations in the Bangkok basin are always greater than those outside the basin by a factor of 3", larger for PGV, "about 4 to 5 times in the horizontal direction".
  - The outside-basin free-field stations named include KCBA and NGPA.
  - Observed Bangkok PGA lies mostly beyond median + 5σ of the GMMs.
  - Long-period amplification is in the 1–3 s and 5–7 s bands.
  - [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-025-02295-7)
- Earlier records (2007–2021, eight events of Mw ≥ 5.5 at more than 600 km) showed basin PGA always > 1 cm/s² and about 3 times amplification. — [Ornthammarath et al. 2023](https://geoenvironmental-disasters.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40677-023-00259-0)
- HVSR fundamental periods for Bangkok-area stations are about 5–7.5 s (TMDA about 5.3 s, PWSA 7.5 s). — search extract of [SRL H/V study](https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/ssa/srl/article-abstract/97/3/1681/728327/Observed-H-V-Responses-Across-Thailand-from-the?redirectedFrom=fulltext); attribution not fully confirmed
- Distance of BKSI (13.7263 N, 100.5598 E) to the SAO site (13.80 N, 100.55 E): **≈8.3 km** (my haversine calculation). PWSA is 7 km from BKSI per BEE, so it is likely within about 15 km of SAO, but its exact distance is unknown. Distances for PTNA, KMUA and BKKA cannot be computed, since their coordinates were not found. — coordinates: [TMD supplementary](https://earthquake.tmd.go.th/documents/file/Supplementary_Info_PatinyaPornsopin2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- The SAO site, Chatuchak near Kamphaeng Phet 2 Rd, lies in the north-central basin, between "central" PWSA/BKSI and "northern" PTNA. All three recorded about 0.02 g, so the SAO free-field PGA was very likely about 0.02 g (≈15–25 cm/s²), PGV about 10–14 cm/s, with dominant long periods. Values of 0.08 g or more are inconsistent with every instrumental record.
- For the SAO building, which has a long fundamental period, spectral acceleration near its period matters more than PGA. The amplified bands at about 1–3 s and 5–7 s are the relevant ones. PWSA is the best available proxy record, and the BEE paper and the Maruyama report give its spectra.

### Gaps
- No instrument at or within about 5 km of the SAO site was found.
- No site-specific Vs30 or site class for the SAO site, or for each TMD station, was found. The BEE extract's "NEHRP C" statement is questionable for the Bangkok clay sites.
- The KCBA and NGPA outside-basin PGA values were not retrieved.
